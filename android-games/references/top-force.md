## Target decision

Choose T3's **active commander + local base reinforcement** loop, keeping the commander, soldiers, base and enemy fire together in one close overhead scene. It is concrete enough to build without silently substituting a generic world-map military strategy game. Retain the gun/equipment queue, short coin-spend feedback and escalation from infantry to vehicles, while treating advertised 29-second scaling as compressed promotion.

## First prototype boundary after interpretation review

Prove **one directly controlled commander → one front → local equipment queue → visibly equipped reinforcements affect that front → one vehicle escalation**, with win/loss/retry. Keep base and combat in one readable scene. Defer three fronts, medics, ammunition logistics, carts and dragged blueprints until the simple reference loop is enjoyable. These are original expansion ideas, not ad-fidelity requirements. Do not set campaign/unit quotas before prototype appeal is established.

## Candidate full-game design and deferred ideas

- **Camera/style:** portrait angled overhead 3D; follow commander within a bounded local battlefield. Base gates and approaching force should stay within one screen whenever possible. Original teal rescue brigade versus rust drone army, stylized chunky silhouettes, visible recoil/bolts/trails and snow/stone terrain; no reference soldiers, flags, logos, character art or copied level.
- **Control:** left floating stick moves commander, auto-fire in range while moving; optional right aim/priority tap and short dodge. At base equipment stations, hold contextual Supply/Build to spend scrap or deliver a capped stack. Drag station blueprint onto marked pads between waves; tap stat preview. The exact input model is our interpretation, not an observed original rule.
- **Repeated loop:** shoot a nearby threat, collect scrap, return through gate, choose gun station/barracks/vehicle workshop, equip queued troops and dispatch them, then personally help the weakest front. Incoming waves make choosing when to leave the front meaningful. Building and combat share one world without recurring menu interruption.
- **Decision depth:** three fronts, limited station footprint and shared scrap. Rifle squads hold light enemies, anti-armor crews need ammunition delivery but counter vehicles, medics rescue wounded troops at opportunity cost. A workshop vehicle consumes two infantry slots and cannot enter narrow alleys. Commander can escort a vulnerable resupply cart or defend a gate; troops still fight when commander leaves. Supply travel and limited simultaneous deployment create tradeoffs instead of endless currency inflation.
- **Mission design:** 12 missions, 4 unit roles, 3 support buildings, 6 enemy roles and 3 bosses; 40–60-minute initial arc. First missions 2–3 minutes, later missions 4–5. Introduce one station/counter at a time; finale uses two simultaneous fronts and a moving enemy artillery unit. Every mission has a visible objective: hold, escort, break siege or recover foundry.
- **Progression:** fixed victory rewards unlock sidegrade loadouts and station variants. Replays offer mastery medals, not compulsory income grind. Replenishment occurs from explicit in-mission resources; no training timers, exhausted energy, real-world production or online assaults while absent.
- **Win/loss/retry:** hold HQ integrity and achieve mission objective; zero HQ or commander health loses. Retry starts with same selected kit and seed; pre-wave checkpoint optional. Battle recap identifies gate damage and supply starvation. Full campaign ends with foundry liberation and unlocks challenge mode.
- **Replay/presentation:** seeded skirmishes, alternate front layouts, limited-supply challenges, wave forecast and readable supply/health indicators. Shake/particles optional; original weapon audio with sound slider. Avoid overwhelming coin bursts that hide incoming attacks.

## Acceptance

1. Early/middle/final captures match T3's local base + direct commander + visible reinforced soldiers and vehicles. A passive upgrade city or detached arena shooter fails.
2. Demonstrate equipment queue actually changing a soldier's role, an anti-armor counter, supply shortfall affecting battle, two viable station layouts, objective win, commander/base loss and immediate retry. All effects remain legible at phone size.
3. Rule tests for station costs/occupancy, troop queue, ammunition consumption, deployment cap, scrap collection, target priority, objective outcomes and exact retry/save restoration. Units cannot become permanently stranded at gates.
4. Offline Android launch, lifecycle interruption, atomic versioned save, export/import, update preservation and touch accuracy; measure heaviest encounter frame time on phone and review fun separately from functional checks.
